How the system works

One study. Two concepts. Two very different outcomes.

A research team is running a multi-site cardiovascular outcomes study. Both hospitals use the same OMOP concept codes. Standard data quality checks show no problems. Here is what the analysis found before the study ran.

The study

Multi-site cardiovascular outcomes study examining heart failure and comorbid Type 2 Diabetes, two hospital networks

Type 2 Diabetes is highly prevalent among heart failure patients and is included as a key comorbidity in this outcomes analysis. Both hospitals participate in the same OMOP research network and have passed data quality checks. The study team is ready to begin pooled analysis across both sites.

Analysis results for the two concepts in scope

Heart Failure — Reduced Pumping Function

Hospital ARequires a heart scan to confirm the diagnosis before coding. The scan objectively measures how well the heart is pumping.
Hospital BTakes the doctor's clinical judgment. A scan is not routinely required before coding the diagnosis.
The problemSame concept code. One hospital requires objective proof. The other does not. Pooling them produces a misleading result.
What happensThe study query is blocked. A qualified reviewer must document how to handle the difference before the study proceeds.

The resultA score of 0.0005. Effectively zero difference between the two sites.
What happensThe query is cleared immediately. The research team can pool Type 2 Diabetes data without adjustment.

What this meant for the study

The research team knew about the heart failure gap before spending a dollar on analysis. The study design was adjusted before it started.

Type 2 Diabetes was cleared immediately. No time wasted reviewing a concept already consistent across both sites.

The reviewer's decision was recorded in a tamper-evident audit log. Documented, dated, and defensible.

No patient data left either hospital at any point. The analysis compared summary profiles only.

The problem

Data quality checks will not catch this

Both hospitals coded correctly. Both passed every data quality check. The problem is not bad data. It is that two institutions apply the same concept differently in practice. That difference silently distorts your study.
